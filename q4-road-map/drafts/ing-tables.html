Data Platform Roadmap · 10d

These are the actual tables in the platform's PostgreSQL database, taken from the code. On the left is what to load: a connection to the source, a target for the output, and a pipeline that joins them, with one pipeline_objects row for every table or file the pipeline moves. Connections and targets never hold passwords; they point to an encrypted secret by reference. In the middle is what each run did. A job is one run of a pipeline, full or incremental. It is split into job_tasks, one per table or file, and that is the row the whole run lifecycle on the previous slide works on: its status, its attempt number, rows and bytes read and written, the watermark it reached and the files it wrote. Each task writes a job_events row for every phase, its validation results comparing source and target, and the watermark for the next incremental load; job_metrics keeps the throughput. On the right, in orange, are the five tables and two columns the Q4 proof of concept added in one migration: schedules with a cron and a time zone per pipeline, alert channels and deliveries for Teams or Slack, and reconciliation specs and runs that compare our output with the legacy pipeline during the parallel run. The API writes the configuration and starts jobs, the Celery workers write everything about a task, and the scheduler writes schedules, retries, alerts and reconciliation. Every row carries a tenant ID, which keeps one client's data away from another's.
